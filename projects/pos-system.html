Point-of-Sale System for a Working Store

Built a touchscreen checkout system that a real store uses today. It logs every sale as structured data and produces daily, weekly, and monthly sales reports, plus automatic monthly PDF reports and backups.

  • Python
  • Tkinter
  • JSON
  • ReportLab
  • Serial
  • ESC/POS

The problem

A store needed a simple checkout that worked with hardware it already had (a customer pole display, an Epson receipt printer, and a touchscreen laptop) and no barcode scanner.

What I built

  • A touchscreen keypad interface (Tkinter) with automatic decimal entry (type 2-4-9 → $2.49) for faster checkout.
  • Live totals on the customer display over a serial connection; receipts printed via ESC/POS.
  • Every sale saved as a structured record: transaction ID, timestamp, line items (name, price, qty), total.

The data side

  • Built-in reporting: all-time, today, this week, this month, and a month-by-month breakdown.
  • One-click PDF financial reports (ReportLab).
  • On the 1st of each month it automatically backs up the transaction log and generates a fresh report, at most once per month.
  • Validation that keeps reports trustworthy: malformed or hand-edited records are repaired on load, missing totals are rebuilt from line items, and sales with no usable date are counted separately instead of being silently dropped.

Why it matters

Reports are only as good as the data behind them. This project taught me to build for data quality at the point where data is captured.
